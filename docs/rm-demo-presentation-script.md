# RM Workspace — Live Demo Script

> **How to use this:** Everything in normal text is meant to be **read aloud, word for word**. Anything in `[ square brackets ]` is a stage direction — a click, a pause, or where to point — *not* to be spoken. Each section opens with a one-line goal so you can find your place at a glance.
>
> **Total runtime:** ~10–12 minutes + Q&A. You can drop the "Switch to Mohammed" act if you're tight on time.

---

## Pre-flight checklist (do this before they're in the room)

- [ ] Logged in as the **Relationship Manager** role.
- [ ] Sitting on the **RM Workspace** page (the client list), not a sub-page.
- [ ] Open **Sarah Chen** and **Mohammed Ali** once each beforehand so their AI Briefings are warm and load instantly — no spinner in front of the client.
- [ ] Browser zoom at a level the room can read from the back. Close other tabs.
- [ ] Have this script on a second screen or phone.

---

## ACT 1 — The opening (≈60 seconds)

> *Goal: set the problem before you show the product.*

"Thank you all for the time. What I'm going to show you today is the screen your front-line teams would actually live in.

[Quick note to set expectations.] One thing before we start — you'll see the role labelled 'Relationship Manager' on screen, but treat that as a placeholder. This same experience could just as easily be a teller, a call-centre agent, or any customer-facing role. The title isn't the point — the capability is.

Here's the problem we set out to solve. When a valuable customer calls or walks in, your front-line team is usually flying blind for the first two or three minutes — pulling up the core system, the card system, the support tickets, the statements, all in separate windows — while the customer waits. By the time they have context, the conversation has already gone cold.

[PAUSE]

So we built one screen that does the prep *for* them — before the customer says a word. Let me show you."

---

## ACT 2 — The portfolio cockpit (≈2 minutes)

> *Goal: show that the RM sees their whole book, prioritised, the moment they log in.*

[You're on the RM Workspace list page. Gesture across the four cards at the top.]

"This is what the relationship manager sees the second they log in. Straight away — across the top — how many clients are in their portfolio, the total assets they're responsible for, how many of those clients are flagged as **at-risk**, and how many actions are waiting on them today.

[POINT to the colored stat cards.] Notice it's colour-coded — green for the money they manage, red for risk, amber for what needs doing. They're reading their whole morning in one glance.

[POINT to the 'My Clients' list, and specifically the colored left edges of the rows.] And down here is their actual book of clients. See the coloured edge on each name — gold means a VIP or premium client, red means at-risk. So before they've clicked anything, their eye goes straight to who matters and who's in trouble.

[POINT to the search bar at the top right of the 'My Clients' card.] They can search the whole portfolio by name right here.

[POINT to the 'Next Best Actions' panel on the right.] And on the right, the system has already ranked what they should be doing — the highest-priority conversations float to the top. Nobody has to decide what to work on first; the system already did.

Now — let's open a real client and see how deep this goes."

---

## ACT 3 — Open Sarah Chen: the 360 view (≈90 seconds)

> *Goal: show the consolidated single-customer view and the action bar.*

[CLICK Sarah Chen.]

"This is the full 360 on Sarah Chen. One screen, everything about the relationship.

[POINT to the header.] Up top — who she is, her segment, her KYC status, how long she's been with the bank.

[POINT to the action buttons top-right: Call / Email / Draft Outreach.] And right here, the RM can act immediately — call her, email her, or draft an outreach message — without leaving the page or copying her details into another tool.

[POINT across the five KPI tiles.] Underneath, her financial snapshot at a glance — total balances, what she owes, money coming in, money going out, and what she spends most on. Notice the colours do the thinking again: green is money in, red is money out, amber is what she owes. An RM reads her entire financial position in about three seconds.

[PAUSE, then gesture down the page.] Below this we've got her accounts, her cards, her loans, her open support tickets, and her recent transactions — all on one screen. No more hopping between five systems.

But here's the part I really want you to see."

---

## ACT 4 — The AI Briefing (the first 'wow' — ≈2 minutes)

> *Goal: this is the headline moment. Slow down and let it land.*

[Make sure you're on the **AI Briefing** tab.]

"This — [POINT to the AI Briefing card] — is the briefing the AI prepared on Sarah **before** the RM even opened her file.

[Read the top summary paragraph aloud, or paraphrase it live so they see it's specific to her.] It's read everything — her transactions, her support history, her products — and written a plain-English summary of where this relationship stands right now.

[POINT to the three coloured insight cards.] Then it boils it down to three things the RM actually needs:

- [POINT to 'Key Concern'.] **The key concern** — what's likely bothering her right now.
- [POINT to 'Next Best Action'.] **The next best action** — what to do about it.
- [POINT to 'Opportunity'.] **And the opportunity** — how to grow the relationship at the same time.

[PAUSE — this is the line to land.] So before the customer has said a single word, the RM walks in already knowing what's wrong, what to do, and what to offer. That's the difference between a reactive bank and a proactive one.

[If a Conversation Digest is visible, POINT to it.] It's even summarised her recent conversations with the bank, so the RM picks up exactly where the last person left off."

---

## ACT 5 — Draft Outreach (≈45 seconds)

> *Goal: show the AI doesn't just advise — it does the work.*

[CLICK the **Draft outreach** button on the briefing.]

"And it doesn't stop at advice. Watch — [the outreach draft appears] — it's written the actual outreach message for the RM, built around that opportunity, in the right tone and the right currency for this market.

The RM reads it, tweaks a word if they like, and sends. What used to be a ten-minute task is now ten seconds."

[Close the dialog.]

---

## ACT 6 — Ask the Copilot (the second 'wow' — ≈2 minutes)

> *Goal: show natural-language, customer-scoped answers — not generic chat.*

[CLICK the **Ask Copilot** tab.]

"Now, the briefing answers the obvious questions. But an RM always has follow-ups. So we gave them a copilot that knows *this specific customer*.

[CLICK one of the suggested starter prompts — e.g. the 60-second snapshot of Sarah.] I'll just ask it for a quick snapshot of Sarah.

[Let it stream the answer.] Notice this isn't a generic chatbot. Every answer is grounded in *her* data — her balances, her transactions, her history — and it explains its reasoning, so the RM can trust it and, just as importantly, repeat it to the customer with confidence.

[Optionally fire a second prompt about her highest-priority risk.] I can ask anything — what's her biggest risk, what should I sell her next, how do I position it — and it answers as if it knows her personally. Because it does."

---

## ACT 7 — The reveal: same questions, different customer (≈2 minutes)

> *Goal: prove this is real context adaptation, not a canned script. This is the closer.*

[CLICK 'Back to Dashboard', then CLICK **Mohammed Ali**.]

"Here's the test that separates a real system from a demo trick. Let me open a completely different customer — Mohammed Ali — and ask the **exact same questions**.

[POINT to the briefing as it loads.] Straight away, a different story. Sarah's briefing was about service and optimisation. Mohammed's is about [read his key concern / next action live] — because he's a different person with a different financial life.

[CLICK Ask Copilot, fire the **same** snapshot prompt you used for Sarah.] Same screen. Same RM. Same question I just asked about Sarah.

[Let it answer.] And a completely different, specific answer — grounded in *his* business, *his* cash flow, *his* risks.

[PAUSE — land it.] That's the whole point. This isn't a script that says the same thing to everyone. It genuinely reads each customer and adapts. Whichever of your clients an RM opens, they get a briefing built for that one person."

---

## ACT 8 — The close (≈60 seconds)

> *Goal: translate the wow into business value and hand back to the room.*

"So let me bring it back to what this means for the business.

**Operationally** — your front-line teams walk into every conversation prepared. Faster prep, better first contact, fewer issues escalating because someone missed a warning sign.

**Commercially** — every single customer interaction now comes with a relevant, personalised opportunity already teed up. You're growing the relationships you already have.

And **for the customer** — they feel known. They're not repeating themselves, not waiting while someone digs through five systems. That's the experience that keeps your best clients loyal.

[PAUSE.] This is the relationship manager's cockpit — proactive, personalised, and built around one customer at a time. I'd love to hear your questions."

## Backup answers for Q&A

**"Is the AI making up these insights?"**
> "No — every insight is grounded in that customer's actual data: their transactions, balances, products, and support history. And notice the answers explain their reasoning, so they're auditable, not a black box."

**"What happens if the AI is down or slow during a real customer interaction?"**
> "The system is built to degrade gracefully — if the live AI is unavailable, it falls back to pre-prepared notes so the RM is never left with a blank screen in front of a customer."

**"Does this work across markets and currencies?"**
> "Yes. The whole experience adapts to the market — currency, local payment rails, local context. The same workflow runs for a UAE client and a Kenyan client; the data and the language follow the customer."

**"How would we roll this out?"**
> "Start with the relationship-manager workflow you've seen here, then extend the same customer-context engine to tellers and customer-service teams. The underlying 360 model is the same; we're just surfacing it to more roles."

**"Where does the customer data come from?"**
> "It consolidates the systems you already have — core banking, cards, lending, support — into one view. We're not asking you to replace anything; we're putting a smart, unified layer on top."

## Wow-moment cheat sheet (the four lines that must land)

1. **Before the customer speaks** — *"The RM already knows what's wrong, what to do, and what to offer."* (Act 4)
2. **It does the work** — *"What used to be a ten-minute task is now ten seconds."* (Act 5)
3. **It knows this customer** — *"It answers as if it knows her personally. Because it does."* (Act 6)
4. **Same question, different customer** — *"This isn't a script that says the same thing to everyone. It genuinely reads each customer."* (Act 7)
